Design decisions — video hero

What's different from a static photo background, and why each choice was made

01

Dual-gradient cinematic overlay

Left side darkens to 0.88 opacity so white text is always legible. Right side opens to near-transparent so the cat in motion gets full visual weight. Static photos don't need this — video has depth that earns more openness.

02

Film grain texture

An SVG feTurbulence filter at 4.5% opacity gives the frame an editorial, cinematic quality. Animated at 0.4s steps to simulate real grain movement. This detail signals craft without screaming for attention.

03

Pill header: 0.28 opacity vs 0.52

Against a static image, the pill needs 0.52 opacity to read. Against video, motion provides its own depth — 0.28 is enough and looks significantly more premium. The blur (14px) does the heavy lifting.

04

Word-by-word headline reveal

Each word clips out of a hidden overflow container with 0.12s stagger. Feels like a film title card. The em words ("feline coworker") fade to 72% white to add rhythm without a secondary colour hit.

05

Trust micro-line between the CTAs

"Free shipping · 30-day guarantee · Ships in 1–2 days" sits in the dead centre space between the two text CTAs at 10px tracked caps, 30% white. Answers the last micro-objection exactly where the hand hovers before clicking.

06

Mute toggle (28px, bottom-left)

Small frosted-glass circle. Video is muted by default. The icon signals that sound exists — the choice to unmute belongs to the user. Appears last in the entrance sequence so it doesn't compete with the headline.

07

Scroll breath indicator

SCROLL in 5.6px tracked caps + a dot that loops downward. Appears 2.2s in after all other elements have settled. Disappears on first scroll via IntersectionObserver. Doesn't compete — it arrives only when the scene is complete.

08

Video progress bar (2px, bottom edge)

Ultra-thin white line at 35% opacity that tracks the video loop. Shows the video has a finite duration — subconsciously encourages the user to keep watching. Doubles as a subtle bottom boundary for the hero.
